# Terrain methods: slope, aspect, elevation

Status labels used below: **implemented** (code exists and ran), **tested** (a check ran and its result is quoted), **estimated** (arithmetic from measurements, not run).

## 1. What exists

Two terrain tiers, kept separate (docs/ARCHITECTURE.md 3.3).

1. Tier 2, analytical (implemented, tested; the pilot and 14 1x1 degree mountain cells, section 10). `pipeline/terrain` reads the current USGS 3DEP 1/3 arc-second DEM, reprojects it to a 10 m UTM 13N grid, computes slope and aspect, and writes static products per region:
   1. Display: classified slope raster tiles. `terrain-slope-pilot.pmtiles` (5.3 MB, z10 to z15) and one `terrain-slope-<cell>.pmtiles` per cell (z10 to z14, 4.2 to 18.5 MB, 154.2 MB for all 14).
   2. Numeric inspection: lossless elevation blocks from which the browser reproduces the exact analytical elevation, slope and aspect of the tapped cell. `terrain-numeric-pilot.pmtiles` (7.7 MB), `terrain-numeric-n40w106-{nw,ne,sw,se}.pmtiles` (12.0 to 15.7 MB each, 55.8 MB) and, from the proof run, `terrain-numeric-n40w107-{nw,ne,sw,se}.pmtiles` (14.8 to 15.9 MB each, 62.6 MB).
2. Tier 1, browser (implemented as pure functions in `app/lib/terrain-math.js`, tested in node, compared against Tier 2 below). Slope and aspect computed in the browser on the Web Mercator grid from Terrarium-encoded terrain tiles: Mapterhorn (WebP, 512 px) per the amended contract, with AWS Terrain Tiles (PNG, 256 px) as the fallback provider. Visual and approximate. `mercatorPixelSize` and `mercatorRowLatitude` take the tile size, so 512 px tiles need no other change.

Coverage: display for 14 1x1 degree cells, lon -109 to -104 for lat 37 to 38 and lon -108 to -105 for lat 38 to 41 (134,901 km2, every Colorado range with ski terrain; the cells extend into New Mexico and Wyoming at the edges). Numeric for n40w106 (lon -106 to -105, lat 39 to 40: Front Range from Berthoud Pass to Kenosha Pass, Evans, Grays and Torreys), n40w107 (lon -107 to -106, lat 39 to 40: Vail Pass, Gore and Tenmile Ranges, Independence Pass, Aspen) and the pilot (EPSG:26913 414110, 4381320, 447020, 4419360, inside n40w106 and n40w107). Outside these cells there is no Tier 2 data.

Product naming (for app routing; every product has a sibling `<id>.meta.json` with `id`, `kind`, `url`, `bytes`, `bbox` [west, south, east, north], `coverage` (GeoJSON Polygon), `sources` and, for display, `minzoom`, `maxzoom`, `classes`):

1. Display: id `terrain-slope-<region>`, kind `terrain-display`, url `./data/terrain/terrain-slope-<region>.pmtiles`, where `<region>` is `pilot` or a 1x1 degree cell name `nNNwWWW` (NW corner, as 3DEP). A cell archive draws only inside its cell (west and north edges inclusive), so all cell archives can be loaded together without overlap; `bbox` is the cell. The pilot archive overlaps cells n40w106 and n40w107 with the same classes (all but at most 8 of 12.5 million cells, section 10.3); do not draw both (use the cell archives, or the pilot alone).
2. Numeric: id `terrain-numeric-<cell>-<part>` with `<part>` in `nw`, `ne`, `sw`, `se` (0.5 degree quarters), kind `terrain-numeric`, url `./data/terrain/<id>.pmtiles`; `bbox` is the quarter. `regionPartName(lon, lat)` in `terrain-math.js` returns `<cell>-<part>` for a point. `terrain-numeric-pilot` is redundant with the n40w106 and n40w107 parts (same values except one cell, section 10.3).

## 3. Analysis grid and reprojection

1. Grid: EPSG:26913 (NAD83 / UTM 13N), 10 m cells, edges on multiples of 10 m, shared with the avalanche-model lane so products co-register exactly. NAD83 to NAD83 means no datum shift is involved.
2. Buffer: the grid is enlarged by 16 cells (160 m) on every side, the DEM is reprojected and slope/aspect computed on the enlarged grid, then the buffer is cropped. Every published cell, including the outermost row and column, therefore has a full 3x3 neighbourhood of real data. Pilot result: 0 NoData cells of 12,518,964.
3. Resampling: bilinear. The source pixel (7.9 x 10.3 m) is close to the 10 m target, so bilinear interpolates between the four nearest source samples without the overshoot of cubic kernels (which can invent ridges and pits next to cliffs) and without the blockiness of nearest neighbour (which duplicates or drops source columns at this size ratio and adds false slope steps). Averaging was rejected because the target is not much coarser than the source. GDAL settings: exact per-pixel coordinate transform (`tolerance=0`), plain 4-point kernel (`XSCALE=1`, `YSCALE=1`; by default GDAL widens the kernel by a scale it estimates per warp chunk, which made neighbouring regions disagree by up to 0.1 m), float64 arithmetic.
4. Quantization: the reprojected DEM is rounded to 0.1 m (`round(z * 10) / 10`, float64) before slope and aspect. This is part of the analysis definition, not a display step: it lets the numeric product store exact integer decimetres and the browser rebuild bit-identical slope and aspect (section 7). Effect on slope: quantization noise has a standard deviation of 0.029 m per cell, which moves Horn slope by about 0.07 degrees on gentle terrain and 0.035 degrees at 45 degrees, far below the DEM's own error.
5. NoData: source -999999 is masked before resampling; any analysis cell without valid source data is NaN internally and -9999 in GeoTIFFs. Any 3x3 window that touches NoData gives NoData slope and aspect (no edge extrapolation).
6. Tile overlap: where 3DEP tiles overlap, the tile list is sorted by name and the first valid value wins, so neighbouring regions always take the same value.
7. Pixel alignment: the window read from the COGs is expanded onto the exact 3DEP lattice (multiples of 1/10800 degree) and mosaicked at exactly 1/10800 degree, so source pixels are copied without any sub-pixel shift and every region assigns a pixel the same position. The first pilot build lacked this. Its mosaic sat 0.38 pixel east-west and 0.27 pixel north-south off the 3DEP grid, and that displaced the whole DEM about 3.0 m east and 2.8 m south (measured by least squares on the before and after difference: 3.04 m, 2.84 m). This was found during the Tier 1 work and fixed at 2026-11-06T00:57Z. The lattice snapping and warp settings were finalized at 01:23Z; all results in this document come from that final build.
8. Seams (tested): the pilot and cell n40w107 overlap in a strip 53 columns wide (x 414110 to 414640, 201,612 cells). DEM, slope and aspect are identical in all 201,612 cells, although the two regions were computed from different mosaics on different grids.
9. Work-dir outputs (not committed): `<region>_dem_26913_10m.tif`, `<region>_slope_26913_10m.tif`, `<region>_aspect_26913_10m.tif` (float32, NoData -9999) and `<region>_dem_buffered_26913_10m.tif`, for `pilot` and `n40w107`.

## 4. Slope and aspect

1. Horn (1981) on a 3x3 window `a b c / d e f / g h i` (first row north): `p = ((c + 2f + i) - (a + 2d + g)) / (8 dx)` (dz/d east), `q = ((a + 2b + c) - (g + 2h + i)) / (8 dy)` (dz/d north), slope = atan(sqrt(p^2 + q^2)) in degrees, grid aspect = atan2(-p, -q) in degrees clockwise from grid north, wrapped to [0, 360). Aspect is the direction the slope faces (downslope). The same expression order is used in Python (`terrain_core.py`) and JavaScript (`terrain-math.js`).
2. Cell size: dx = dy = 10 m, the true projected cell size. Grid distance differs from ground distance by the UTM scale factor (0.99963 to 0.99969 in the pilot) and by height above the ellipsoid (about +0.055 % at 3,500 m). Together these change tan(slope) by under 0.1 %, at most 0.03 degrees at 45 degrees. Not corrected.
3. Flat: slope below 1.0 degree gets aspect -1. A direction on a slope under 1 degree is noise at 10 m resolution and meaningless for skiing.
4. True north (amended contract): published aspect is degrees clockwise from TRUE north. The grid aspect is corrected cell by cell: true aspect = (grid aspect + gamma) mod 360, where gamma is the meridian convergence at the cell centre, computed from the cell's UTM easting and northing with the Krueger inverse series (Karney 2011, n^3 terms; `meridian_convergence_deg` in Python, `utmConvergenceDeg` in JS, same operation order). gamma is negative west of the central meridian: in the pilot it runs from -0.39 to -0.65 degrees (-0.52 at the centre), so true aspect is about half a degree less than grid aspect; in western Colorado it reaches about -2.7 degrees. Flat (-1) and NoData pass through unchanged. Checked against PROJ (`get_factors().meridian_convergence`) at 7 Colorado points: within 1.6e-10 degrees; a finite-difference test confirms the sign (a step due true north has grid bearing -gamma). Over the pilot, our true aspect minus GDAL's grid aspect averages -0.518 degrees, as expected.
5. Tier 1 aspect from Web Mercator is already relative to true north (Mercator meridians are vertical), so Tier 1 and Tier 2 aspects now share a reference.

## 5. Slope classes and colors

1. Classes (degrees, lower bound inclusive, upper exclusive): `<25` (transparent), `25-30`, `30-35`, `35-40`, `40-45`, `>=45`. A value of exactly 30.0 is in `30-35`. Classes describe steepness only.
2. Colors (also in each display `meta.json`, for the legend): 25-30 `#F7D13D`, 30-35 `#FB9B06`, 35-40 `#ED6925`, 40-45 `#BC3754`, >=45 `#56106E`. The hue path runs yellow, orange, red, crimson, purple (the familiar steepness convention), and CIELAB lightness falls monotonically: L* 84.9, 72.2, 60.1, 44.3, 21.9, at least 12 units between neighbours. Because the order is carried by lightness, the classes stay ordered and distinguishable under protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia and in greyscale. `tests/pipeline/test_terrain_core.py` asserts the lightness rule. Over a hillshade, layer opacity below about 0.6 erodes the lightness separation; the app should default to 0.6 or more.
3. Shares (Tier 2): pilot 34.7 % of cells at 25 degrees or steeper, 20.3 % at 30 or steeper, 1.0 % at 45 or steeper, maximum 83.1 degrees; n40w107 rectangle 24.3 % at 25 or steeper, 14.7 % at 30 or steeper, maximum 83.9 degrees.

## 6. Display product (terrain-slope-<region>.pmtiles)

1. Web Mercator 256 px PNG tiles (8-bit palette, index 0 transparent through tRNS), PMTiles v3, tile compression none. Each output pixel takes the class of the analysis cell under the pixel centre (nearest neighbour), so class edges stay crisp and no new values are invented. Slopes under 25 degrees, NoData and outside-coverage pixels are transparent; fully transparent tiles are omitted (the pmtiles 4.5.0 MapLibre protocol returns `data: null` for a missing raster tile).
2. Zoom range z10 to z15. Ground pixel at 39.75 N: z13 14.7 m, z14 7.35 m, z15 3.67 m. At z14 a 10 m cell covers 1.36 pixels, so cells alternate between one and two pixels wide and class edges look jagged; z15 gives 2.7 pixels per cell and faithful edges. Above z15, MapLibre overzooms z15 (`raster-resampling: nearest` keeps edges crisp). Below z10 a 10 m product is not legible. Measured sizes: pilot z10 to z15 5.30 MB (z15 alone 2.67 MB). Cell n40w107 z10 to z14 16.3 MB (z13 4.22 MB, z14 9.95 MB, 2,731 z14 tiles); adding z15 would make it 33.9 MB (z15 alone 17.6 MB, 10,324 tiles), over the 25 MB file limit, so cells stop at z14 (`display_max_zoom` in `config.yaml`) and the pilot keeps z15.
3. A 1x1 degree cell's archive draws only pixels whose centre lies in its own cell (west and north edges inclusive), so neighbouring archives never overlap and layer opacity stays uniform across cell edges.
4. Tested: pilot, 1,200 random pixels (400 each at z12, z14, z15) read back from the archive and decoded as PNG: 1,200 of 1,200 match the class of the analysis cell under the pixel centre. Cell n40w107: 1,000 random pixels inside the cell (z12, z14) match 1,000 of 1,000; 200 pixels just outside the cell edges are all transparent.

## 7. Numeric inspection product (terrain-numeric-<region>[-<part>].pmtiles)

1. Goal: a tap returns the analytical elevation, slope and aspect of the 10 m cell under the point, not a value reverse-engineered from display colors.
2. Design: store only the quantized elevation on the analysis grid itself, and let the browser recompute Horn slope and aspect from the 3x3 window with the same arithmetic. Because the Tier 2 rasters were computed from exactly these decimetre values (section 3.4), the browser result equals the analysis value.
3. Tiling (not Web Mercator): tile (x, y) at PMTiles zoom 9 is the block of 256 x 256 analysis cells whose top-left corner is (x * 2560, 4700000 - y * 2560) in EPSG:26913, plus a 1-cell apron on every side (258 x 258 values), so every 3x3 window lies inside one tile. Never add this archive as a map source.
4. Encoding per tile: integers n = round(z * 10) (elevation = n / 10 m), NoData code -1000000; 2-D first differences (row then column), zigzag to unsigned, the four bytes of each uint32 written as four separate planes, zlib (RFC 1950) level 9. Lossless with respect to the 0.1 m analysis DEM. No image format, so no alpha premultiplication, color management or canvas involvement. Decoding uses only `fetch`, `DecompressionStream('deflate')` (Safari 16.4+) and integer arithmetic.
5. Browser API (`app/lib/terrain-math.js`): `numericCellAt(lon, lat, meta.numeric)` returns `{z, x, y, col, row, cellE, cellN}` or null outside the grid (lon/lat to UTM 13N with a Krueger n^4 series, within 1 mm of PROJ at 7 Colorado test points; `cellE`, `cellN` are the cell centre); `decodeNumericTile(bytes, 258)` returns an Int32Array; `inspectNumericCell(elev, cell, meta.numeric)` returns `{elevationM, slopeDeg, aspectDeg}` with null for NoData and -1 for flat, aspect relative to true north (it applies the same convergence correction as the pipeline, at the cell centre). The decoder parameters are in the archive metadata (`numeric`) and the sibling `meta.json`. GPS and MapLibre coordinates are WGS84; they are treated as NAD83 (difference about 1 m in Colorado, a tenth of a cell).
6. Why this design: measured on the pilot DEM, bytes per cell were 1.60 for 24-bit RGB PNG at 0.01 m, 1.02 for RGB PNG at 0.1 m, 0.95 for this encoding at 0.01 m and 0.58 at 0.1 m. Storing slope and aspect as well would roughly double or triple the size and still need floor-quantization tricks to keep classes consistent. Web Mercator numeric tiles were rejected: a Mercator pixel centre is not the cell under the tap, so the value would come from a neighbouring cell near edges, and z14 to z15 needs 1.9 to 7.3 times more pixels than the 10 m grid.
7. Split: a 1x1 degree cell is written as four 0.5 x 0.5 degree archives, `<cell>-nw`, `-ne`, `-sw`, `-se`, each holding every block that touches its part, to stay under 25 MB per file. `regionPartName(lon, lat)` in `terrain-math.js` names the archive for a point (west and north edges inclusive, centre lines belong to the south and east parts).
8. Tested (`verify_numeric.py`, node 26.7.0 + pmtiles 4.5.0 reading the archives from disk with the same `getZxy` path as the browser), last run 2026-11-06T01:26Z (pilot) and 01:28Z (n40w107) on the final build:
   1. Pilot: 1,004 points (1,000 uniform random plus the four corner cells). Elevation, slope and true-north aspect equal the GeoTIFF float32 values at 1,004 of 1,004; slope class and flat flag equal at all 1,004.
   2. n40w107: 1,004 points uniform in the cell plus its four corners, each routed to its part archive by `regionPartName` (239 to 267 points per part): equal at 1,004 of 1,004 for elevation, slope, aspect, class and flat flag.
   3. Against a float64 Python recomputation the largest differences were 7.1e-15 degrees (slope) and 1.1e-13 degrees (aspect), i.e. last-bit differences between V8 and the C math library.
9. Size: pilot 7.70 MB (224 tiles, 0.62 bytes per cell). n40w107: 62.6 MB in four parts of 14.8 to 15.9 MB (414 tiles each; about 5 % of blocks are stored twice along the part lines, and the UTM rectangle extends past the cell).

   So the browser method itself loses little at z13 to z14; the large AWS errors are the stale source.
7. Recommendation: where Tier 2 exists, the app uses Tier 2 numbers and says so. Elsewhere, compute browser slope from Mapterhorn at z13 (the contract zoom). Its slope agreement with Tier 2 is within about 0.1 degree of z12, and z13 matches the 10 m analysis grid better than z14 does. Trust level on slopes over 25 degrees: typical difference from Tier 2 about 1.2 degrees (RMSE 1.9 degrees, 95 % within 4 degrees), the same 5-degree class about four times in five, within one class 99 %. Display it to whole degrees, labelled "computed in the browser from Mapterhorn terrain tiles". If the app falls back to AWS, label it as approximate from a pre-2018 DEM and expect errors of 4 to 6 degrees and the right class only one time in three. That is good enough to see where steep terrain is, not to read a slope angle.
8. `terrain-math.js` needed no change for 512 px tiles: `mercatorPixelSize(z, lat, tileSize)` and `mercatorRowLatitude(z, yPixel, tileSize)` already take the tile size. The browser decodes Mapterhorn WebP through `createImageBitmap` and canvas. The tiles are lossless and opaque, so no alpha premultiplication applies. The canvas decode path has not been tested in a browser by this lane.

## 11. Uncertainty and limitations

1. The DEM is bare earth and smoothed: 3DEP 1/3 arc-second resampled to 10 m cannot resolve features narrower than about 20 to 30 m (cliff bands, couloirs, gully walls, cornices, convex rollovers). Real short pitches are often steeper than the 10 m slope, and 3x3 Horn smoothing lowers peaks of slope further. Snow depth and wind loading change the skiable surface.
2. 3DEP vertical accuracy is reported by USGS per source project; it is metre-scale, larger in steep and forested terrain. A 1 m error between neighbouring cells 20 m apart changes slope by up to about 3 degrees. Treat class boundaries as uncertain by a few degrees.
3. The numeric inspector reproduces the analysis exactly; it does not make the analysis more accurate than the DEM.
4. Aspect is relative to true north and is undefined (-1) under 1 degree. It describes the 10 m surface, so small-scale aspect changes inside gullies and on rolls are lost.
5. Slope classes and colors describe steepness only. They are not an avalanche hazard rating, do not mark terrain as safe or unsafe, and say nothing about current conditions.
